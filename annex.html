Annex — 2025–2026 Annual Report Employment Examples

This annex supports discussion of Employment Equity Act modernization and the Canadian Multiculturalism Act through ten institution-reported examples of inclusive employment practices. The summaries identify the employment barrier or equity objective, the institutional response and the reported result. Selection prioritizes recruitment, retention and advancement, particularly for Indigenous, Black and other racialized people, while retaining the broader beneficiary groups identified in the submissions.

These examples illustrate practices relevant to employment equity and multicultural inclusion. They do not establish that the initiatives resulted from legislative modernization, demonstrate compliance with either Act or prove legislative impact. Activities, participation figures and reported benefits are distinguished from measured employment outcomes. Community labels follow the submissions and are not a statement of statutory designated-group status.

Original responses and source identifiers are provided in SOURCES.md.

Parks CanadaYouth participating through Parks Canada and partner organizationsYouth Employment and Skills Strategy (YESS)Youth facing barriers, including women, Indigenous youth, racialized youth and youth with disabilitiesTo improve access to employment for youth facing barriers, Parks Canada delivered employment opportunities and work experience through YESS. In 2025–2026, it reported supporting 1,356 youth, including 696 hired internally and 660 through partner organizations; 77% identified as belonging to employment equity groups. The example illustrates inclusive recruitment and entry into employment, including for Indigenous and racialized youth. The response does not establish longer-term retention or advancement outcomes.
Canada Border Services AgencyIndigenous candidates, including candidates in remote communitiesNational Officer Recruitment Program — Indigenous recruitmentIndigenous communitiesTo reduce recruitment barriers affecting Indigenous candidates, including those in remote communities, CBSA made its fitness assessment more accessible and undertook targeted outreach. Its Prairie Region participated in more than 28 Indigenous recruitment activities and events. CBSA reported that these efforts contributed to an increase in regional Indigenous representation from 7.9% to 9.11%. The example connects changes to recruitment practices with reported representation gains; the response does not isolate the effect of individual measures.
Public Service Commission of CanadaBlack executives and aspiring Black leaders in the federal public serviceExecutive Leadership Development Program for Black Leaders; Career Counselling and Coaching Program for Black Public ServantsBlack public servantsTo advance equitable access to leadership development for Black public servants, the Personnel Psychology Centre provided culturally informed assessment, coaching and counselling. In 2025–2026, it supported two cohorts of 25 Black executives and provided career counselling to nearly 200 aspiring Black leaders across four cohorts. Participants reported clearer strengths, career goals and development priorities. The example illustrates targeted access to career-development supports; promotions or changes in leadership representation were not quantified.
Shared Services CanadaIndigenous IT apprentices and program graduatesIndigenous Information Technology Apprenticeship ProgramIndigenous participantsTo address barriers to Indigenous participation and career advancement in IT, SSC’s Indigenous Circle provided apprentices with technical peer support, mentoring, weekly wellness check-ins and management intervention when challenges arose. SSC continued to offer employment to graduates, who could access the IT Professionals Development Program. The example connects practical workplace supports with entry into employment and continued development. The response does not specify the number of graduates or employment offers.
Public Services and Procurement CanadaEmployees receiving language scholarships; Black professionals participating in peer learningSecond Official Language Scholarship Program; Jùmọ̀ SọrọBlack employees and other equity-group employees, including Indigenous peoples, persons with disabilities, women and members of the 2SLGBTQIA+ communityTo improve equitable access to official-language learning and career development, PSPC awarded 19 individualized language-training scholarships, including 14 supported through an agreement with the Task Force for Black Public Servants for Black employees. Separately, Jùmọ̀ Sọrọ provided supportive peer language practice for 80 Black professionals. The example illustrates targeted development support for employees facing advancement barriers. Participation figures are not added together because overlap is unknown; subsequent promotions were not reported.
Fisheries and Oceans CanadaEmployees in the Black Public Servants and Employment Equity cohortsSecond Official Language Training (SOLT) ProgramBlack public servants and employees from designated employment equity groupsTo address barriers to language training and career advancement for designated employment equity groups, DFO combined formal language instruction, mentorship and self-directed learning. In 2025–2026, 61 employees participated: 20 in the Black Public Servants cohort and 41 in the Employment Equity cohort, with ten mentors supporting the latter. Participants reported improved language skills, confidence and readiness for advancement. The example links equitable access to development with career progression requirements, without establishing subsequent promotion outcomes.
Canada Revenue AgencyHigh-potential employees participating as protégésSponsorMe PlusIndigenous Peoples, Black employees and visible minority employeesTo expand diversity in executive leadership, CRA’s SponsorMe Plus offered high-potential employees coaching, access to high-priority projects and advocacy for developmental opportunities. During 2025–2026, 28 protégés self-identified as Indigenous Peoples, Black or visible minority employees. The example illustrates targeted sponsorship and access to career-building assignments as approaches to equitable advancement. The response reports participation, not the number subsequently appointed to executive roles.
Business Development Bank of CanadaIndigenous employees supported throughout their career journeyIndigenous Career PathwaysIndigenous PeoplesTo support Indigenous employees throughout recruitment, onboarding, development, advancement and retention, BDC implemented Indigenous Career Pathways. The framework combines culturally informed onboarding, buddy and mentorship programs, regular career check-ins and partnerships with Indigenous organizations and educational institutions. BDC reported stronger Indigenous talent pipelines and support for career progression. The example illustrates culturally responsive support across the employment cycle; hires, promotions and retention rates were not quantified.
Canada School of Public ServiceIndeterminate or term employees in the targeted language-training cohortInternal Second Language Training Program — targeted cohortIndigenous, Black and other racialized employees; employees with disabilities; 2SLGBTQIA+ employeesFollowing audit findings on employment equity representation in acting appointments, the School launched a targeted language-training cohort for Indigenous, Black and other racialized employees, employees with disabilities and 2SLGBTQIA+ employees. Sixteen employees received three weeks of full-time, one-on-one training following the August 2025 call for nominations. The example shows how representation findings informed targeted access to a skill relevant to advancement. The response does not establish a subsequent change in acting appointments or promotions.
Canadian Food Inspection AgencyHigh-potential Black employees at EX-equivalent, EX-minus-1 and EX-minus-2 levelsBlack Employees Leadership Development ProgramBlack employeesTo address systemic barriers to Black employees’ advancement into senior leadership, CFIA launched a 12-month program for employees at EX-equivalent, EX-minus-1 and EX-minus-2 levels. It combined leadership training, coaching, sponsorship and career planning with in-person and virtual delivery. CFIA reported early improvements in leadership competencies, networks and engagement. The example illustrates structured support for equitable advancement and inclusion in leadership; subsequent promotions were not quantified.
