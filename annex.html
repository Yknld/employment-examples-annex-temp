Annex — 2025–2026 Annual Report Employment Examples

Selected institution-reported examples. Each institution appears once. Source references and original responses are provided in SOURCES.md.

Parks CanadaYouth participating through Parks Canada and partner organizationsYouth Employment and Skills Strategy (YESS)Youth facing barriers, including women, Indigenous youth, racialized youth and youth with disabilitiesIn 2025–2026, Parks Canada reported supporting 1,356 youth: 696 hired internally and 660 through partner organizations. Of participants, 77% identified as belonging to employment equity groups. The program provided work experience and opportunities to develop skills and career pathways.
Canada Border Services AgencyIndigenous candidates, including candidates in remote communitiesNational Officer Recruitment Program — Indigenous recruitmentIndigenous communitiesCBSA adapted its fitness assessment to improve access for candidates from remote communities. In the Prairie Region, it participated in more than 28 targeted Indigenous recruitment activities and events. The response reported an increase in regional Indigenous representation from 7.9% to 9.11% and identified these efforts as contributing to that increase.
Public Service Commission of CanadaBlack executives and aspiring Black leaders in the federal public serviceExecutive Leadership Development Program for Black Leaders; Career Counselling and Coaching Program for Black Public ServantsBlack public servantsIn 2025–2026, the Personnel Psychology Centre supported two cohorts of 25 Black executives at EX-01 to EX-03 with assessments, individualized coaching and development support. It also provided assessment and counselling services to nearly 200 aspiring Black leaders across four cohorts. Participants reported clearer strengths, career goals and development priorities.
Shared Services CanadaIndigenous IT apprentices and program graduatesIndigenous Information Technology Apprenticeship ProgramIndigenous participantsSSC’s Indigenous Circle supported apprentices with technical peer support, mentoring, weekly wellness check-ins and management intervention when challenges arose. SSC continued to offer employment to graduates, who could access the IT Professionals Development Program for further career development. The response describes a pathway from apprenticeship into employment and continued development, without specifying the number of graduates or employment offers.
Public Services and Procurement CanadaEmployees receiving language scholarships; Black professionals participating in peer learningSecond Official Language Scholarship Program; Jùmọ̀ SọrọBlack employees and other equity-group employees, including Indigenous peoples, persons with disabilities, women and members of the 2SLGBTQIA+ communityPSPC reported awarding 19 individualized language-training scholarships, including 14 supported through an agreement with the Task Force for Black Public Servants for Black employees. Separately, its Black Professional Network’s Jùmọ̀ Sọrọ program supported 80 Black professionals through informal second-language practice linked to career development. These participation figures are not added together because overlap is unknown.
Fisheries and Oceans CanadaEmployees in the Black Public Servants and Employment Equity cohortsSecond Official Language Training (SOLT) ProgramBlack public servants and employees from designated employment equity groupsDuring 2025–2026, 61 employees participated: 20 in the second Black Public Servants cohort and 41 in the inaugural Employment Equity cohort. The program combined formal language training, mentorship and self-directed learning, with ten mentors supporting the Employment Equity cohort. Participant feedback described improved language skills, confidence and readiness for advancement.
Canada Revenue AgencyHigh-potential employees participating as protégésSponsorMe PlusIndigenous Peoples, Black employees and visible minority employeesDuring 2025–2026, SponsorMe Plus included 28 protégés who self-identified as Indigenous Peoples, Black or visible minority employees. The program develops leadership capacity through coaching, access to high-priority projects and advocacy for development opportunities, with the aim of expanding diversity in executive leadership.
Business Development Bank of CanadaIndigenous employees supported throughout their career journeyIndigenous Career PathwaysIndigenous PeoplesBDC implemented a framework spanning recruitment, culturally informed onboarding, development, advancement and retention. Supports included buddy and mentorship programs, regular career check-ins and partnerships with Indigenous organizations and educational institutions. BDC reported stronger Indigenous talent pipelines and support for career progression; the response does not quantify hires, promotions or retention rates.
Canada School of Public ServiceIndeterminate or term employees in the targeted language-training cohortInternal Second Language Training Program — targeted cohortIndigenous, Black and other racialized employees; employees with disabilities; 2SLGBTQIA+ employeesFollowing findings on employment equity representation in acting appointments, the School launched a targeted call for nominations in August 2025. Sixteen employees benefited from three weeks of full-time, one-on-one language training to acquire and maintain second-official-language proficiency, supporting equitable access to development and advancement opportunities.
Canadian Food Inspection AgencyHigh-potential Black employees at EX-equivalent, EX-minus-1 and EX-minus-2 levelsBlack Employees Leadership Development ProgramBlack employeesCFIA launched a 12-month cohort program combining leadership training, coaching, sponsorship and career planning, delivered in person and virtually. The Agency reported early improvements in leadership competencies, professional networks and participant engagement. The initiative prepares participants for senior leadership opportunities; the response does not quantify subsequent promotions.
