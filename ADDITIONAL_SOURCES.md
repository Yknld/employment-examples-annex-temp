## Row 1 — Fisheries and Oceans Canada

**Initiative:** 2025–2033 Inuit Employment Plan

**Response:** `Sa433711e-R0051-Q19-2` — Q19, answer slot 2; source row 51; field `f179`.

### Original response

DFO advanced the objectives of Article 23 of the Nunavut Agreement through implementation of the 2025–2033 Inuit Employment Plan (IEP), which aims to increase Inuit representation across the Department's Arctic Region and strengthen Inuit participation in the federal public service. Through community outreach, culturally responsive recruitment and staffing practices, career development initiatives, mentorship, leadership development opportunities, and support provided through the Indigenous Career Navigator Program (ICNP), DFO worked to address barriers affecting Inuit employment and advancement. The IEP also identified systemic challenges including limited awareness of federal career opportunities, complex staffing processes, education and training gaps, housing and childcare pressures, and the need for more culturally responsive workplaces and established actions to address them. While Inuit representation remains below long-term objectives, particularly in professional, scientific, and leadership positions, the plan has strengthened pathways for Inuit recruitment and career development while supporting greater inclusion of Inuit knowledge, perspectives, and lived experience in departmental programs, services, and decision-making.
 Canadian Multiculturalism Act Objectives advanced include section 3.1c) promote full and equitable participation and 3.1g) promote institutions that are respectful and inclusive.

## Row 2 — Canadian Security Intelligence Service

**Initiative:** Inclusive recruitment, retention and advancement measures

**Response:** `Sa433711e-R0031-Q19-1` — Q19, answer slot 1; source row 31; field `f178`.

### Original response

Ensuring that Canadians of all origins have an equal opportunity to obtain employment and advancement at CSIS. CSIS has advanced its work to remove systemic and other barriers from employment policies and practices. CSIS employees spent significant time on revision of staffing processes and documents in the areas of recruitment, retention and promotion. Recruitment: CSIS continues to expand the reach of the Diversity Recruiter who travels to an increasing number of job fairs across Canada, and in particular, prioritizing proactive outreach to Indigenous candidates. CSIS career fairs are published on our website, social media platforms and local radio stations. Advertising on LinkedIn has been visual and representative of racialized, Black and persons with disabilities. Human resources staff also reach out directly to applicants in all of their diversity to provide support throughout a process that they may find unusually long due to the added requirements related to a top-secret clearance. CSIS is collaborating with ESDC on the IT Indigenous Apprenticeship Program, a significant partnership for our technical branches and Indigenous communities. Self-identification and/or self declaration is encouraged throughout the recruitment process and CSIS workforce analytics are published. Human Resources (HR) staff receive training on unconscious bias in staffing and on ensuring equitable staffing processes and practices. Review of applications from racialized groups and Indigenous candidates is prioritized. HR practitioners have also developed an in-house tool to anonymize CVs before review. A Culturally Relevant Gender-based Analysis Plus (CRGBA Plus) was used to guide a direct to region hiring process aimed at hiring Black, Indigenous, and racialized employees, and in order to gain insight and help inform and mitigate for barriers, specifically for Indigenous employees. This was also shared with Five Eyes Intelligence Agency partners as part of the Five Eyes Indigenous Summit. Although numbers of Indigenous employees remain below our Workforce Availability number (3.6%), there has been a slight improvement (an increase in representation from 2.3% Indigenous employees to 2.4%), and we expect that these processes and practices will continue to help going forward. Advancement and Retention at the Service: Internal staffing processes and opportunities for advancement have also been reviewed and improved; and a new selection decision policy is being put in place. Employees are encouraged to self-declare throughout a career opportunity process. Mentoring programs focused on diverse employees have been initiated, and HR staff may also directly connect candidates with current role holders for mentorship and insights. Development opportunities have also been established specifically for racialized employees. All employees develop personalized learning paths in line with their career goals. Resume guidance, interview preparation help, and actionable feedback are all part of the career opportunity process. The Executive Talent Management Questionnaire (EXTMQ) has been revised to highlight the self-declaration section, with clear communication to ensure Executives understand its purpose and how this information will be used. The data collected from self-declarations plays a critical role in informing Executive movement decisions by enabling employment equity (EE) gap analysis for positions requiring staffing. CSIS continues work to improve retention and has responded to feedback from both the Public Service Employee Survey (PSES) and internal discussions and surveys through processes and programs that work to increase decision making transparency, enhance engagement and ownership of CSIS’ mandate by all employees, and contribute to making the Service a safe and inclusive workplace in which they feel pride and belonging. New governance committees are making minutes available to all employees, security clearance processes continue to be analysed and improved for equity, and employee networks continue to grow to support diverse employees in the workplace. This year CSIS saw the formation of the Employee Engagement and Insights (EEI) team and the publishing of articles that allow diverse employees to see themselves and to learn to understand others

## Row 3 — Communications Security Establishment Canada

**Initiative:** Inclusive recruitment and expanded Sponsorship Program

**Response:** `Sa433711e-R0035-Q19-3` — Q19, answer slot 3; source row 35; field `f180`.

### Original response

Building equitable career opportunities through inclusive recruitment and the Sponsorship Program

 CSE strengthened equitable access to employment, career development, and leadership opportunities through a range of targeted initiatives designed to support underrepresented communities. The organization expanded its Sponsorship Program for Indigenous, racialized, neurodivergent employees, and persons with disabilities, helping participants access leadership development, strengthen professional networks, and advance their careers. CSE also enhanced its recruitment and workforce planning practices through the implementation of equity dashboards, voluntary self-identification tools, and targeted outreach partnerships with organizations such as Obsidi, Indigenous Link, and QueerTech. To drive accountability, CSE established an ambitious objective that three out of every four new hires come from equity-deserving groups and leveraged real-time representation data to monitor diversity throughout the recruitment lifecycle—from application to appointment—and inform evidence-based decision-making.

 CSE continued to advance workplace accessibility by ensuring its career platform supports screen readers and accessibility tagging, while making ongoing improvements to both its internal and external websites. In support of a more inclusive and accessible work environment, the organization is also exploring a pilot project for an offline AI-powered captioning solution designed for use in high-security environments. This technology has the potential to improve accessibility and workplace participation for employees who are Deaf, hard of hearing, or neurodivergent by enhancing comprehension, reducing cognitive load, and supporting sustained engagement. Collectively, these efforts demonstrate that operational security and inclusion are mutually reinforcing objectives. As a result, CSE continued to strengthen workforce representation across equity-deserving groups, including Indigenous Peoples, racialized employees, persons with disabilities, and 2SLGBTQIA+ employees. Notably, employees who identify as persons with disabilities represent 14.1% of the workforce, exceeding workforce availability rates in Canada.

## Row 4 — Royal Canadian Mounted Police

**Initiative:** Indigenous Pre-Cadet Training Program and Diverse and Inclusive Pre-Cadet Experience

**Response:** `Sa433711e-R0094-Q19-3` — Q19, answer slot 3; source row 94; field `f180`.

### Original response

The RCMP offers two recruitment programs: the Indigenous Pre-Cadet Training Program (IPTP) and the Diverse and Inclusive Pre-Cadet Experience (DICE) program. IPTP promotes equitable access to policing careers for First Nations, Inuit, and Métis Peoples, increasing Indigenous representation within the organization which fosters a workforce that reflects Canada’s diversity. Through targeted training, mentorship, and recruitment supports the program helps reduce barriers to participation and enhances cultural understanding, which strengthens the RCMP’s ability to serve diverse communities across Canada. DICE is a recruitment and retention initiative focused on removing systemic barriers that impact racialized and underrepresented groups who aspire to join the RCMP. Through a network of RCMP mentors, located throughout the country, the program provides direct assistance and mentorship to pre-cadets through all stages of the recruitment process. The DICE program also helps shift community perceptions and attitudes toward federal policing while also creating pathways for Black individuals to enter the RCMP. In fiscal year 2025-26, two DICE troops were held.

## Row 5 — Canadian Food Inspection Agency

**Initiative:** Action Plan to Support Black Employees

**Response:** `Sa433711e-R0021-Q19-1` — Q19, answer slot 1; source row 21; field `f178`.

### Original response

1) Success 1: The Agency strengthened its commitment to anti‑racism and equitable treatment in the workplace through the development of the CFIA Action Plan to Support Black Employees, a targeted, multi‑year initiative informed directly by employee engagement and external research. The plan was shaped by insights from Dr. Rachel Zellars’ study on the Black Executive Community in the Federal Public Service, as well as candid discussions between senior leadership and the Black Employee Network (BEN), where employees shared lived experiences of systemic barriers and inequities. Developed collaboratively by BEN, its executive champion, and the People and Culture Branch, the action plan reflects a deliberate and structured response to these findings, aligning with broader commitments such as the Clerk’s Call to Action on Anti‑Racism, Equity and Inclusion and the Agency’s Equity, Diversity and Inclusion Framework. In doing so, the Agency advances the objectives of the Canadian Multiculturalism Act, particularly by promoting policies and practices that enhance equal opportunity for employment and advancement and by strengthening inclusion and respect for diversity within Canadian society. The collaborative, employee‑driven approach also supports the objective of enhancing the ability of individuals and communities of all origins to contribute to the evolution of Canada.

 The action plan establishes a comprehensive framework to address systemic barriers and improve outcomes for Black employees across the organization. Measures include embedding clear anti‑racism and equity objectives into executive performance agreements to strengthen accountability; enhancing the use of disaggregated data to identify and address representation and advancement gaps; expanding access to official language training and leadership development opportunities; and supporting career progression through mentorship, sponsorship, and targeted talent initiatives. The use of disaggregated data directly supports the objective of collecting information to inform responsive and inclusive policies and programs, ensuring that decisions are evidence‑based and reflective of Canada’s multicultural reality. The plan also prioritizes creating safe, independent spaces for employees to raise concerns and access culturally informed supports without fear of reprisal, reinforcing a workplace culture that is sensitive and responsive to diversity, while enabling the Agency to better leverage diverse perspectives and cultural understanding. As a result, the Agency has strengthened accountability for anti‑racism at the leadership level, improved access to development opportunities for Black employees, and established the foundation for more equitable representation and advancement across the organization. Early outcomes include increased employee engagement and trust, more informed decision‑making using established data sources, and a more inclusive workplace environment where systemic barriers are actively identified and addressed.

## Row 6 — Environment and Climate Change Canada

**Initiative:** 2025–2030 Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Strategy

**Response:** `Sa433711e-R0046-Q19-1` — Q19, answer slot 1; source row 46; field `f178`.

### Original response

ECCC launched its 2025-2030 Diversity, Equity and Inclusion Strategy, a five-year departmental roadmap built on four pillars: advancing equity in hiring, retention and career development; strengthening education, awareness and engagement; enhancing data; and ensuring consequential accountability and governance. Developed through extensive employee consultations and aligned with the Call to Action on Anti-Racism, Equity, and Inclusion, the Strategy embeds 18 concrete actions with measurable targets, establishing a structured foundation for sustained equity gains across the Department. Action 7 in the DEI Strategy addresses harassment, discrimination and hate, including anti-Black racism, anti-Indigenous racism, antisemitism and Islamophobia. This initiative primarily advanced objective (f), generally carrying on activities in a way that is sensitive and responsive to Canada's multicultural reality, and objective (a), ensuring equal opportunity for employment and advancement, by giving the Department a clear, accountable framework to significantly reduce underrepresentation.

## Row 10 — Defence Research and Development Canada

**Initiative:** Targeted Defence Scientist recruitment and Employment Equity student staffing initiative

**Response:** `Sa433711e-R0041-Q19-1` — Q19, answer slot 1; source row 41; field `f178`.

### Original response

In FY2025/26, DRDC incorporated Employment Equity (EE) inclusions in hiring processes and launched a partially qualified pool for Defence Scientists (DS-03) with disabilities, following a targeted EE hiring process run in FY24/25. Additionally, DRDC continued efforts toward enhancing diversity and inclusivity within our organization through the Employment Equity (EE) student staffing initiative. This promotes opportunities for qualified students who self-declare as members of EE groups. To support data collection on student staffing, hiring managers are asked to submit a form for all student staffing actions. The data collected indicated an increase in the EE criteria used in student staffing from ~40% in FY2024/25 to ~70% in FY25/26.
