### Original response

The Youth Employment and Skills Strategy at Parks Canada
 Parks Canada’s delivery of the Youth Employment and Skills Strategy (YESS) is a strong example of advancing inclusive employment and equitable access to opportunities for youth across Canada. Parks Canada’s Staffing Centre of Expertise manages the YESS program and is the only participating federal organization that uses funding for direct youth hires rather than solely through grants and contributions.
 In the 2025–2026 reporting period, Parks Canada supported 1,356 youth through YESS, including 696 hired internally and 660 through partner organizations. Of these participants, 77% identified as belonging to employment equity groups, including women, Indigenous youth, racialized youth, and youth with disabilities.
 Canadian Multiculturalism Act objectives advanced:
 • Equal opportunity for employment and advancement
 • Enabling communities of all origins to contribute to Canada’s continuing evolution
 • Supports the collection and use of disaggregated data on participation and representation, enabling evidence-based approaches to inclusive recruitment and workforce planning.
 Community impact:
 The program provides meaningful work experience to youth facing barriers, helping build skills, confidence, and career pathways. At the same time, it supports Parks Canada in strengthening workforce diversity and fostering a more inclusive and representative organization.

 The return of the Learn-to Camp program at Rocky Mountain House National Historic Site
 During the 2025-2026 reporting year, Parks Canada’s Learn-to-Camp program returned to Rocky Mountain House National Historic Site representing a strong example of Parks Canada advancing inclusion, cultural awareness, and access to heritage experiences.
 The program welcomed first-time visiting newcomer Canadians from a wide range of cultural backgrounds, including participants from India, China, Colombia, Ethiopia, Eritrea, Somalia, Yemen, and Pakistan. For many, this was their first experience visiting a Parks Canada place and their first time camping in Canada. Through hands-on activities such as setting up tents, cooking outdoors, and learning about wildlife safety, participants gained the confidence to independently explore Canada’s natural spaces in the future.
 The program also integrated opportunities for cultural learning and dialogue. Participants engaged with a Métis Elder and learned about Indigenous histories and perspectives, fostering greater awareness and understanding of Canada’s diverse cultural heritage.
 Several Canadian Multiculturalism Act objectives advanced:
 § Enabling communities of all origins to contribute to Canada's continuing evolution
 § Enhancing understanding of and respect for diversity
 § Generally carrying on activities in a way that is sensitive and responsive to Canada’s multicultural reality
 Community impact:
 This initiative helped reduce barriers to participation in outdoor and heritage experiences for newcomers and diverse communities. It strengthened participants’ sense of connection to nature and Canadian history, increased confidence in accessing Parks Canada places, and contributed to greater cultural awareness and inclusion. The program also supported organizational goals related to increasing visitation among diverse audiences and fostering long-term engagement with Canada’s natural and cultural heritage.

Objective C
 The CBSAs National Officer Recruitment Program has improved its recruitment process by removing barriers to participation by Indigenous communities. As an example, it has adapted its process to make the fitness assessment more accessible, enabling candidates from remote communities (many Indigenous) to participate. In addition to this, targeted outreach activities have been used to strengthen the Agency’s relationship with Indigenous communities by recognizing their talents and strengths.

 As a specific example, within the Prairie Region, the CBSA participated in over 28 targeted Indigenous recruitment activities and events which included trade shows, pop-up recruitment booths, virtual information sessions, community events such as the Manitoba Métis Federation Annual General Assembly, Buffalow Point First Nation’s Summer Safety and Awareness event etc. These efforts have helped to increase Indigenous representation within the region from 7.9% to 9.11%.

Advancing culturally informed leadership development for Black public servants Objectives advanced: (a) Equal opportunity for employment and advancement (c) Enhancing understanding of and respect for diversity (f) Carrying on activities in a manner that is sensitive and responsive to Canada's multicultural reality The Personnel Psychology Centre (PPC) supported initiatives under the Treasury Board Secretariat’s Black Public Servants Action Plan to advance equitable access to leadership development opportunities. In 2025–2026, the PPC contributed specialized assessment, counselling and coaching services to support Black public servants aspiring to leadership roles. Through the Executive Leadership Development Program for Black Leaders (ELDP-BL), the PPC supported two cohorts of 25 Black executives at the EX-01 to EX-03 levels. Participants received structured feedback on psychometric assessments, individualized coaching and development support focused on strengthening self-awareness and leadership capacity. Survey results showed high satisfaction, with all participants indicating they were satisfied or very satisfied with the program overall. The PPC also delivered the Career Counselling and Coaching Program for Black Public Servants (CCC-BPS), providing specialized assessment and counselling services to nearly 200 aspiring Black leaders across four cohorts. Participants reported that the program helped them identify strengths, career goals and development priorities in a safe and culturally informed environment.

### Original response

PSPC invested in second official language learning through a range of formal and informal initiatives across branches. In addition to departmental programs, several branches offered their own learning opportunities to support employees in developing and maintaining their second official language skills, including virtual and group training, self-directed learning, one-on-one learning, and workplace language practice. Two notable initiatives focused on supporting employees from employment equity groups. Firstly, through the PSPC Second Official Language Scholarship Program, PSPC awarded a total of 19 individualized language training scholarships to employees from employment equity groups. A Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the Treasury Board of Canada Secretariat’s Task Force for Black Public Servants supported 14 of the 19 scholarships for Black employees. The other five scholarships were awarded to employees who self-identified as persons with disabilities, Indigenous peoples, women, members of the 2SLGBTQIA+ community, and Black employees. In addition, PSPC’s Black Professional Network hosted Jùmọ̀ Sọrọ, an informal second-language practice program that supported the career development of 80 Black professionals by providing opportunities to strengthen their second official language skills in a supportive peer-learning environment. Together, these targeted initiatives, alongside branch-led language learning efforts, demonstrated PSPC’s ongoing commitment to fostering a bilingual and inclusive workforce by providing employees from employment equity groups with equitable access to language learning and development opportunities.

**Response:** `Sa433711e-R0051-Q19-3` — Q19, answer slot 3; source row 51; field `f180`.

### Original response

DFO launched a Second Official Language Training (SOLT) Program to address barriers that may limit equitable access to language training and career advancement opportunities for designated employment equity groups. During 2025–26, 61 employees participated in the program, including 20 participants in the second Black Public Servants cohort and 41 participants in the inaugural Employment Equity cohort. Participants received formal language training, mentorship, and self-directed learning opportunities, while the Employment Equity cohort benefited from support provided by ten mentors at the supervisory, managerial, and executive levels. The expansion of the program created targeted opportunities for employees from underrepresented groups to develop official language competencies that are often required for career progression in the federal public service. Participant feedback highlighted increased confidence, improved language skills, and enhanced readiness for advancement. Feedback from participants and mentors highlighted the program's impact on language development, confidence, and career growth:
 "Overall, I found the most valuable aspect of the program has been the 1:1 instructor-led training and I look forward to obtaining a better oral score during my oral exam!" — SOLT Participant
 "While mentoring provides participants with valuable guidance and answers, these meetings also gave me, as a mentor, the opportunity to reflect on various situations I encountered throughout my career." — SOLT Mentor
 Canadian Multiculturalism Act Objectives advanced include section 3.1c) promote full and equitable participation and 3.1i) encourage recognition and use of languages while strengthening participation and inclusion.
