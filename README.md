# Annex — 2025–2026 Annual Report Employment Examples

Ten selected examples from the supplied 2025–2026 Q19 responses. Selection prioritizes concrete employment relevance, complete information and reported scale or results. This is a qualitative selection, not a ranking of institutional performance. Beneficiaries are described at the level identified in the response; individual names are not required. All results are institution-reported.

| Federal Institution | Recipient / Beneficiary | Initiative | Communities Served | Summary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parks Canada | Youth participating through Parks Canada and partner organizations | Youth Employment and Skills Strategy (YESS) | Youth facing barriers, including women, Indigenous youth, racialized youth and youth with disabilities | In 2025–2026, Parks Canada reported supporting 1,356 youth: 696 hired internally and 660 through partner organizations. Of participants, 77% identified as belonging to employment equity groups. The program provided work experience and opportunities to develop skills and career pathways. [Source 1](SOURCES.md#source-1) |
| Canada Border Services Agency | Indigenous candidates, including candidates in remote communities | National Officer Recruitment Program — Indigenous recruitment | Indigenous communities | CBSA adapted its fitness assessment to improve access for candidates from remote communities. In the Prairie Region, it participated in more than 28 targeted Indigenous recruitment activities and events. The response reported an increase in regional Indigenous representation from 7.9% to 9.11% and identified these efforts as contributing to that increase. [Source 2](SOURCES.md#source-2) |
| Public Service Commission of Canada | Black executives and aspiring Black leaders in the federal public service | Executive Leadership Development Program for Black Leaders; Career Counselling and Coaching Program for Black Public Servants | Black public servants | In 2025–2026, the Personnel Psychology Centre supported two cohorts of 25 Black executives at EX-01 to EX-03 with assessments, individualized coaching and development support. It also provided assessment and counselling services to nearly 200 aspiring Black leaders across four cohorts. Participants reported clearer strengths, career goals and development priorities. [Source 3](SOURCES.md#source-3) |
| Federal Economic Development Agency for Southern Ontario | Foodpreneur Lab; Black and diverse entrepreneurs in the food and beverage sector | Foodpreneur Lab incubator and accelerator programming — Regional Innovation Ecosystem program | Black entrepreneurs and other underrepresented communities | FedDev Ontario reported a $2 million non-repayable contribution supporting Foodpreneur Lab’s incubator and accelerator programming. The programming helps Black and diverse entrepreneurs address systemic barriers, access business supports and grow their enterprises. This example concerns entrepreneurship and economic participation; the response does not quantify jobs created. [Source 4](SOURCES.md#source-4) |
| Public Services and Procurement Canada | Employees receiving language scholarships; Black professionals participating in peer learning | Second Official Language Scholarship Program; Jùmọ̀ Sọrọ | Black employees and other equity-group employees, including Indigenous peoples, persons with disabilities, women and members of the 2SLGBTQIA+ community | PSPC reported awarding 19 individualized language-training scholarships, including 14 supported through an agreement with the Task Force for Black Public Servants for Black employees. Separately, its Black Professional Network’s Jùmọ̀ Sọrọ program supported 80 Black professionals through informal second-language practice linked to career development. These participation figures are not added together because overlap is unknown. [Source 5](SOURCES.md#source-5) |
| Fisheries and Oceans Canada | Employees in the Black Public Servants and Employment Equity cohorts | Second Official Language Training (SOLT) Program | Black public servants and employees from designated employment equity groups | During 2025–2026, 61 employees participated: 20 in the second Black Public Servants cohort and 41 in the inaugural Employment Equity cohort. The program combined formal language training, mentorship and self-directed learning, with ten mentors supporting the Employment Equity cohort. Participant feedback described improved language skills, confidence and readiness for advancement. [Source 6](SOURCES.md#source-6) |
| Canada Revenue Agency | High-potential employees participating as protégés | SponsorMe Plus | Indigenous Peoples, Black employees and visible minority employees | During 2025–2026, SponsorMe Plus included 28 protégés who self-identified as Indigenous Peoples, Black or visible minority employees. The program develops leadership capacity through coaching, access to high-priority projects and advocacy for development opportunities, with the aim of expanding diversity in executive leadership. [Source 7](SOURCES.md#source-7) |
| Natural Resources Canada | Equity-deserving employees receiving development grants and mentorship | Visible Minority Advisory Council Employee Development Fund and Executive Mentorship Program | Equity-deserving employees | In 2025–2026, the Employee Development Fund awarded 13 grants from 52 applications, while the Executive Mentorship Program connected 23 mentors with 27 mentees. The initiatives supported access to professional development, skills, networks and career opportunities. The response does not provide a demographic breakdown of recipients. [Source 8](SOURCES.md#source-8) |
| Canada School of Public Service | Indeterminate or term employees in the targeted language-training cohort | Internal Second Language Training Program — targeted cohort | Indigenous, Black and other racialized employees; employees with disabilities; 2SLGBTQIA+ employees | Following findings on employment equity representation in acting appointments, the School launched a targeted call for nominations in August 2025. Sixteen employees benefited from three weeks of full-time, one-on-one language training to acquire and maintain second-official-language proficiency, supporting equitable access to development and advancement opportunities. [Source 9](SOURCES.md#source-9) |
| Canadian Food Inspection Agency | High-potential Black employees at EX-equivalent, EX-minus-1 and EX-minus-2 levels | Black Employees Leadership Development Program | Black employees | CFIA launched a 12-month cohort program combining leadership training, coaching, sponsorship and career planning, delivered in person and virtually. The Agency reported early improvements in leadership competencies, professional networks and participant engagement. The initiative prepares participants for senior leadership opportunities; the response does not quantify subsequent promotions. [Source 10](SOURCES.md#source-10) |
